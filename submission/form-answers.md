## Project Title

SaathBuy — Rural Collective Buying Network

## Brief about your idea

SaathBuy is a proposed platform that helps rural households and small businesses buy together. Users share what they need, their budget, and their preferred delivery date through voice or a simple form. The system groups compatible requests and compares supplier offers after including delivery and service charges. Each buyer sees their own total cost and saving against a comparable individual purchase before approving. If a buyer leaves or an offer changes, SaathBuy recalculates the plan and asks for fresh approval. We will build and evaluate request matching and group-purchase optimisation, supported by local shop or SHG pickup points.

## Detailed explanation of the idea

Problem and theme fit: SaathBuy addresses problem 5.2 under Rural FMCG and Logistics. Individual purchases can be too small to access supplier quantity discounts, while organising a group manually requires matching products, collecting commitments, and dividing costs. A bulk discount is useful only when each buyer benefits after delivery and service charges.

Our solution: Users submit a product, quantity, maximum budget, pickup area, and acceptable date through voice or a simple form. They confirm the interpreted request before it enters a group. SaathBuy matches compatible requests, checks supplier stock and minimum quantities, and proposes a purchase plan. Local shops or SHGs could act as optional collection coordinators under disclosed terms.

Technical approach: This is an Applied AI Systems proposal. Speech and language models will map local product descriptions to a small verified catalogue and ask about unclear units or pack sizes. A constrained optimisation engine will compare eligible groups and supplier offers using total delivered cost. It will respect budgets, quantities, availability, and delivery windows. Exact arithmetic will calculate each share. We will start with explicit requests; demand forecasting will require sufficient historical data.

Buyer control: Every participant will see the product, supplier, full cost breakdown, individual saving, and applicable terms before approving their own purchase. No one commits another person's money. Before supplier confirmation, a changed quote or participant withdrawal creates a new proposal and invalidates earlier approvals. Later cancellations follow the agreed supplier terms. A group with no feasible beneficial offer stays pending or closes.

Prototype and evaluation: We plan one local cluster, a small catalogue, two supplier quote feeds, and one pickup point. We will compare the optimiser with individual purchasing and a simple pooling baseline. Measurements will cover total and per-buyer cost, feasibility, request-matching accuracy, and recovery after cancellations. Synthetic data and simulated supplier or payment actions will be clearly labelled. The interface will save drafts during connectivity gaps; online services will handle quote checks and planning.

Business and impact: We will test a disclosed coordination fee or a subscription for local coordinators. All charges will appear in the buyer's total. The intended benefits are better bargaining power, fewer repeated purchasing trips, and paid local coordination work. These are hypotheses to validate. We are at the idea stage and do not claim deployed users, partnerships, or measured savings.
